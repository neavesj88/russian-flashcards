The four practice modes

The Study tab runs the same cards four ways, and the difficulty ladder between them is the whole point — climb it as a word settles in.

  • Cards — you judge yourself. Fastest, and the only honest mode for brand-new material you have never seen.
  • Choice — four options, and the three wrong ones are pulled from the same deck, so they are thematically close and you cannot win by elimination. Marks the card automatically: right counts as Good, wrong resets it to box 0.
  • Type — always asks you to produce the Russian. This is the mode that actually builds recall. Stress marks, capitals and ё/е are ignored when checking, and a one-letter slip is flagged as a spelling near-miss rather than silently marked wrong. A full ЙЦУКЕН keyboard sits under the box, so no Russian keyboard layout is needed on either phone or PC.
  • Listen — dictation. You hear the Russian and nothing else, and type what you heard. This is the hardest of the four and the one that builds the skill people neglect: recognising a word when it goes past at speed with its vowels reduced. Do it with the Фразы and Предложения decks especially.

Keyboard throughout: 1–4 pick an option, space reveals or continues, Enter submits a typed answer, r repeats the audio.

Using this on your phone

It is one file, so put it anywhere your phone can open it — OneDrive, Google Drive, or emailed to yourself — and open it in the browser. Then Add to Home Screen for an icon that opens it full-screen.

You do not need to install a Russian keyboard. Type and Listen modes have a full ЙЦУКЕН keyboard built in, with space, backspace and ё. On a touch screen the app also asks the browser not to raise its own keyboard, so the two do not fight over the screen.

You do not need to install a language pack either — for the Audio tab. Those are real recordings streamed from МГУ and need nothing installed. The 🔊 speech buttons are the only part that wants a Russian voice, and the app tells you plainly if one is missing rather than going quiet.

Two things worth knowing. Progress is stored per device, so use Progress → Export / Import to move it between phone and PC. And if your phone's browser blocks storage for files opened directly, the app will say so at the top — in that case serve it from your PC instead:

On the PC: python -m http.server 8000 in the project folder, then browse to http://<pc-ip>:8000 from the phone on the same network. That also makes Add to Home Screen behave like a real app.

Audio

Every Russian word, phrase and sentence in the app can be spoken aloud — tap the 🔊 button, or turn on Speak the Russian automatically on the Decks tab. There is a speed control; slow is worth using while you are still decoding the alphabet.

This uses the speech voice built into your device, so it works offline and nothing is downloaded — but it does need a Russian voice present. If the app says audio is off, it will show you exactly where to enable one for Windows, Android or iPhone. A network-based voice is used if that is all the device offers. If you would rather not install anything, skip it: the Audio tab below needs nothing.

A synthetic voice is good enough for stress, rhythm and vowel reduction, which is what you need it for. For real speakers, see the next section.

The workbook recordings

The Audio tab holds 45 native-speaker recordings — the actual audio that the QR codes in Россия — моя любовь: Рабочая тетрадь point to, covering the вводный урок and lessons 1–35. Each one is labelled with its lesson, its exercise number and the workbook page the QR sat on.

This is the material to take seriously. It is real speech at real speed, recorded for these exact exercises. The way to use it:

  • Play a track without looking at the workbook and write down what you hear.
  • Then open the workbook at the page given and check yourself.
  • Then play it again while reading along, and finally shadow it — speak over the top, matching the rhythm.

This is the one part of the app that needs an internet connection — the tracks stream from the МГУ language centre rather than being bundled. Nothing preloads, so opening the tab costs no data until you press play.

Building sentences

The Build tab gives you an English sentence and the Russian words scrambled; you tap them into order. Russian word order is genuinely flexible — it is the endings that carry the grammar, and order mostly signals emphasis — so these are marked against the neutral, natural ordering, and each one names the grammar point it is there to teach.

Grammar drills

The Drills tab is 470 multiple-choice grammar questions across seventeen topics — pronunciation, conjugation, the six cases, adjective agreement, plurals, which case each preposition governs, aspect, verbs of motion, number agreement, нравиться and the dative, в/на/о, gender traps, the spelling rules, past-tense agreement, который, and the instrumental.

Every wrong answer comes with an explanation of the rule, and for conjugation and case items the full paradigm is printed underneath, so a mistake teaches you the whole set rather than just the one form. The distractors are always other real forms of the same word — which is what makes getting it right mean something.

Drills share the scheduler with the vocabulary cards, so an item you get wrong comes back within a few questions and again the next day.

How to use the decks

  • Start with Алфавит, Core words and Verbs. Leave the ten B2 decks switched off until you can read comfortably — 1,270 advanced words will just bury you.
  • Direction matters. Russian→English is recognition and feels easy. English→Russian is recall and is what actually makes you able to speak. Do recognition first on new material, then switch.
  • Rate honestly. "Again" costs you nothing; pretending you knew it costs you the card. Scheduling is a five-box Leitner system: 1 → 3 → 7 → 21 → 60 days.
  • Twenty new cards a day is a sustainable pace. The reviews compound.

Pronunciation — the part most self-teachers skip

Russian spelling is a poor guide to Russian sound. Four processes account for almost the whole gap, and once you know them you can predict the pronunciation of most words you have never heard.

1. Vowel reduction

Only the stressed vowel is pronounced fully. Everything else weakens, on a ladder:

PositionWhat happensExample
Stressedfull, clear vowelдо́м [dom]
One syllable before the stressо and а both → [a] вода́ [vaˈda]
Anywhere else→ [ə], a weak 'uh' мо́локо [ˈmoləkə]
Unstressed е and я→ [i] сестра́ [sisˈtra]

So молоко́ is three different o-sounds: [məlaˈko]. This rhythm — one strong syllable, the rest swallowed — is the Russian accent. Getting it right matters more than perfecting any single consonant.

2. Devoicing and assimilation

  • Voiced consonants at the end of a word go voiceless: хлеб [хлеп], го́род [го́рат], муж [муш].
  • A consonant assimilates to the next one: ло́жка [ло́шка] (devoiced before к), вокза́л [вагза́л] (voiced before з).

3. Hard and soft consonants

Nearly every consonant comes in a hard and a soft version, and the difference is phonemic — брат and брать are different words. A following ь or я/е/ё/ю/и softens; а/о/у/ы/э keeps it hard. Three consonants never change: ж, ш, ц are always hard, ч, щ, й always soft. That is why жи is said [жы].

4. Words that lie about themselves

  • -ого / -его → [-ово / -ево]: его́ [ево́], сего́дня [сево́дня].
  • -тся / -ться → [ца]: учи́ться [учи́ца].
  • что [што], коне́чно [кане́шна].
  • Silent letters: здра́вствуйте [здра́ствуйте], со́лнце [со́нце], по́здно [по́зна].

All of this is drilled in the Произноше́ние deck and the Pronunciation drill topic — and you can hear any of it with the 🔊 button.

Stress

Russian stress is unpredictable and moves within a word's paradigm, and unstressed о is pronounced roughly like a. So хорошо́ comes out closer to kha-ra-SHO. Learning a word without its stress means learning it wrong.

Every word in the A1 decks and the grammar cards here is marked. In the ten B2 decks the mark is present on about a third of entries — the textbook printed stress with an acute accent and the OCR only recovered some of them. Absence of a mark in a B2 deck means unknown, not first-syllable. Check anything you intend to say out loud.

The в/на pair

Both mean roughly "in/on/at/to", and both take prepositional for location but accusative for direction. This one contrast does a great deal of work:

  • Я в теа́тре — I am at the theatre. (prepositional)
  • Я иду́ в теа́тр — I am going to the theatre. (accusative)

Which of в or на a given noun takes is largely lexical and must be memorised: в шко́ле but на рабо́те, на по́чте, на у́лице.

The vowel in the ты form tells you which conjugation you are in: -е-/-ё- means first, -и- means second.

Past tense — easier than you expect

Drop -ть and add -л / -ла / -ло / -ли. It agrees with gender and number, not person: я чита́л (a man speaking), я чита́ла (a woman), они́ чита́ли.

Aspect — the real hurdle

Almost every verb comes as a pair. Imperfective is the process, the repetition, or the bare fact. Perfective is one completed action with a result.

  • Я чита́л кни́гу — I was reading / used to read a book.
  • Я прочита́л кни́гу — I read the book (and finished it).

Consequences worth memorising:

  • The perfective has no present tense — you cannot be in the middle of a finished action. Its present-tense forms are the future.
  • Future imperfective is compound: я бу́ду чита́ть. Future perfective is simple: я прочита́ю.
  • After на́чал, ко́нчил, продолжа́л use the imperfective infinitive.
  • Negative commands are nearly always imperfective: не открыва́йте.

Verbs of motion

A second layer on top of aspect. Unprefixed motion verbs come in pairs that split by direction, not by completion:

Add a prefix and the pair turns into an ordinary aspect pair, with the prefix carrying the direction: при- arrive, у- leave, в- enter, вы- exit, под- approach, от- move away, пере- cross, до- reach, за- drop by, про- pass, по- set off.

Two spelling rules that explain a lot

  • The 7-letter rule. After г к х ж ч ш щ write и, never ы. This is why кни́га pluralises as кни́ги, not книгы.
  • The 5-letter rule. After ж ч ш щ ц write о only under stress, otherwise е. Hence хоро́шего but большо́го.

Most "irregular" endings you meet early are just these two rules firing.

The mistakes English speakers actually make

  • Using «я» when the verb already says it. Russian drops the pronoun far less than Spanish or Italian, so keep it — but never say я есть студент. The present tense of "to be" simply is not used: Я студе́нт.
  • Forgetting that «нравиться» inverts. The thing you like is the subject. Мне нра́вится кни́га = the book is pleasing to me. Plural thing, plural verb: Мне нра́вятся кни́ги.
  • Putting помогать and звонить in the accusative. Both take the dative: Я помога́ю ему́, not его́.
  • Missing the double negative. It is compulsory, not sloppy: Я никогда́ ничего́ не говорю́ — three negatives, all required.
  • Choosing aspect by tense instead of by meaning. Ask "was there a result?", not "was it past?".
  • Using идти́ for a round trip. If you went and came back, it is ходи́л / е́здил.
  • Saying «в работу». A short list of nouns takes на: рабо́та, у́лица, по́чта, вокза́л, заво́д, пло́щадь, plus events like уро́к, ле́кция, конце́рт. And "from" then becomes с, not из.
  • Forgetting the н- after prepositions. у него́, not у его́; с ней, not с ей.
  • Number agreement. 1 → nominative singular, 2–4 → genitive singular, 5+ → genitive plural — and 11–14 go with the 5+ group no matter what they end in.
  • Learning a noun without its gender. Gender decides the adjective, the past tense, and every ending downstream. Learn тетра́дь (f.) as one unit, never bare.

A workable plan

  • Weeks 1–2. Alphabet until reading is automatic. Handwriting is worth the hour — both Антошкина volumes open with прописи (copybook) pages.
  • Weeks 3–12. Антошкина часть 1, one lesson every 2–3 days. Decks: Алфавит, Core words, Verbs. Test yourself with the Белорусская академия tests after lessons 6, 12 and 18.
  • Months 4–8. Антошкина часть 2. Turn on the Numbers/time, Grammar patterns and Verbs of motion decks. Use Матвеев purely to look tables up.
  • Month 9+. Kagan. Switch on one B2 theme deck at a time — start with Работа or Семья — and only add the next when the previous is mostly in box 3 or higher.

Provenance and caveats

The ten B2 decks were extracted automatically from the glossary printed at the back of Kagan/Kudyma/Miller (pp. 419–448). That PDF's text layer is an OCR of italic type, and the OCR confused Latin letters for Cyrillic lookalikes throughout, so the text was repaired programmatically. 1,270 entries were recovered with no parse failures, and spot checks are clean, but a small number of entries may still carry a typo the repair did not catch. Five Russian headwords with obvious OCR damage were corrected by hand against their English glosses.

The A1 decks, the phrases, the sentences, the pronunciation cards, the grammar drills and everything on this page were written for this app. The word list is drawn from a frequency count of the elementary textbooks and the verb list from the lesson-by-lesson syllabus of Антошкина частей 1 и 2. Every drill paradigm is written out in full and machine-checked: each generated question's answer is re-derived from the source paradigm independently of the generator.

Where the recordings came from. The two Антошкина textbook PDFs contain no QR codes — but the Рабочая тетрадь (workbook) does, 45 of them, in the left margin beside every listening exercise. They are not clickable links: they are drawn QR codes, some as vector rectangles and most baked into full-page scans, with no URL anywhere in the file. Each one was located and decoded from the page itself, and the resulting links resolve to the МГУ language centre's audio for that exact exercise.

Two independent extractors were used — one reading the vector rectangles, one sampling the rendered bitmap — and where a code appeared to both, they agreed. Every decoded link returned a real file whose name matches its lesson and exercise, and the 45 filenames come back in the same order as the pages they were found on, which is why the set can be trusted.

Nothing here reproduces the books themselves — the decks are vocabulary and paradigm data for private study.
